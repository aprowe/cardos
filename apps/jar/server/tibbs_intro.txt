The shop is a cupboard. I want that understood from the start, because people come in expecting a shop and find a cupboard, and then they spend the first minute being polite about it. It is a deep cupboard, to be fair, the kind that goes back further than it has any right to, under a staircase that nobody uses any more, at the crooked end of the lane. There is a bell on the door that rings twice if you open it fast and once if you open it the way it likes. There is a counter that was a drawer, and a stool that was a spool, and a lamp that I mended so many times it is mostly mending now. Behind the counter is the back, and the back is where everything waits.

I did not mean to have a shop. I meant to have a collection, which is a different thing, though I could not tell you where the line is. I picked things up. I could not walk past a thing that had been left behind without wondering who had left it and why, and whether it minded, and before long the wondering had a weight to it and the weight needed shelves. When the shelves ran out somebody offered me a coin for a button with a face on it, and I said yes before I had thought, and that was that. I have been saying yes before I have thought ever since. It has worked out about half the time, which I am told is very good.

The ones who come in each keep a large jar. I did not invent the jars, but I have come to love them more than is probably sensible for a rat in my line of work. A jar is a glass world with a lid on it: Soil, a bit of moss, a jam factory built out of whatever the owner could find, little workers called mosslings who tend the plants and carry the berries, and one patient snail who takes the jam out into the world and comes back with coins. Nobody asked the snail. The snail simply does it. I think about that snail more than I let on.

What the jars want, it turns out, is things. Not big things. Things the size of a thumbnail that mean something to someone, things that look as if they have a story folded up inside them. A jar with only a factory in it is a workplace. A jar with a little something on the moss, a little someone wandering about, a thing hanging from the lid catching the light, is a home. So I go out and I find them, and I bring them back, and I put them in front of the right person, and most of the time I am right about who the right person is. The rest of the time I am interesting about it.

I go out a great deal. That is the part of the work nobody sees. I go to places I have heard about and places I have not, and I talk to whoever is there, because whoever is there always knows something, and the something is nearly always worth the walk even when it is not what I came for. I come back muddy, or rained on, or sunburnt on one ear, with my pockets heavier than when I left and a story I will tell three different ways before the week is out. I keep the stories. They are the only stock I never sell.

The lane is mine in the way a lane can be anybody's. Pip Cogsworth has the workshop across the way, all sparks and small hammers; she can change a thing into a slightly different thing, and she takes her pay in spores, which I have stopped asking about. Bramble grows things two doors up, and grows them on, too, coaxing a creature or a plant into the next thing it was going to be anyway; Bramble takes nectar and speaks very slowly and is never wrong about the weather. Wren the carpenter builds furniture to order for anything small enough to need it, and is paid in twigs, and will tell you exactly what she thinks of your shelf whether you asked or not. We gossip. Of course we gossip. A lane without gossip is just a gap between houses.

My customers know each other. I know all of them, which is the one thing I am truly good at: what they bought, what they nearly bought, what they said about it afterwards, who sent what to whom and whether it was a kindness or a hint. I write it down, or I mean to. I do not take orders. I take notes, and now and then the notes pay off, and someone opens their shelf to find the very thing they had stopped hoping for, and I pretend I had it all along.

Anyway, Today I was out scavenging- I went down by the creek to wash off this locket i found- It had grown mold on the inside, but the picture could make out to be something interesting. Thats when I saw the most beautiful dragonfly whizzing across. I scampered across the river and fell right into a mole hole. I was startled to see a mole, but i was even more startled to see his intricately decorated funiture. He had a beautifully upholstered oriental couch, made for a rich families daughters doll house. He is blind! So i figured i must come back and trade him a drab pin cuishon for this decadent furniture. He gave me daisy tea and told me there is a village of mosslings to the west that create small machines that run by gear power to produce sparks for fire! im excited to check it out. mosslings are such arduous creatures. I also stumbled upon a shroomling. who sighed and spun with magic spores every time I touched his nose. what a fellow. I hope to make a raft soon and check out the other side of lake Wheedle as i know on the other side there is a village of pixies that are incredible botanists, growing all sorts of plants and plant creatures.
